title: 'AI and Truth: Is the Era of the Great Information Filter Arriving?'

> **Disclaimer:** The following text is my personal theory and a subjective perspective on current technological trends. The aim of this article is to offer an analytical viewpoint for thought and discussion, not to present irrefutable facts.

In today's article, we will look at one of the most topical subjects: **AI and truth**. AI-generated content began appearing on social media approximately two years ago—first as images, and in recent months, videos have been added. This content is often (not always, but very frequently) of low quality, but that is not the fundamental problem. What we are dealing with—and will continue to deal with—is **fake news**. These have always existed, but AI has helped them gain authenticity, whether through the generation of images and audio or the ability to perfectly target texts using language models. These tools give purveyors of disinformation great power to manipulate the masses.

One consequence of the AI boom that I expect is the emergence of a kind of **content and media filter** on the internet. In my view, this filter should create a deep imaginary chasm between high-quality and low-quality content. High-quality content will include recognized media, creators with significant reach, and authorities such as governments. However, this could cause **strong media centralization**, which is why it will be important to create platforms where ordinary people can create freely. Otherwise, there is a risk of information monopolies and the harsh censorship associated with them.

This filter will also make it significantly harder to break through. If you wanted to succeed, for example, five years ago, your competitors were humans. Today, if you want to establish yourself as a creator (for instance, of short videos), you are no longer just fighting against humans, but against artificial intelligence. The second part of the filter will consist of content that may not necessarily be of low quality but is not "recognized." This will include AI-generated content, so-called "brainrot," but also human creations that failed to gain traction. Most content on the internet will soon belong to this second group, and the share of the verified human footprint will decrease. This phenomenon is partially addressed by the **Dead Internet Theory**.

The existence of this filter may also cause problems for certain platforms. The most threatened, in my opinion, are social networks like Instagram or TikTok, which rely on short videos—the format most frequently generated by AI. If these networks are unable to effectively filter human creation from synthetic content, they may well face **a shift in their basic fundamentals and visions**. These platforms were originally designed as balanced networks where people both consume and create content. With the arrival of AI, however, the **ratio of creators to consumers is rapidly shrinking**.

Personally, I do not think this filter is a negative thing. I perceive it as a relatively positive process that will allow people to consume content created by real human beings. The most difficult challenge will be the recognition itself. Identifying AI content will become increasingly difficult; therefore, the path will likely reverse: **it will be necessary to clearly mark and certify human content**, perhaps with the help of authorities, similar to how books are published.

If you are asking when this process will begin, the answer is: **it has already started**. I cannot pinpoint the exact moment—perhaps it was with the first wave of AI on social media—but I bet you have noticed it too: for example, through the fact that the credibility of information on social media has plummeted over the past year.
